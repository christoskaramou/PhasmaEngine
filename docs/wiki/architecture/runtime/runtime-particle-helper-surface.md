# Runtime Particle Helper Surface

The runtime particle manager owns persistent emitters plus short-lived burst helpers. Lua still exposes the editable emitter surface (`particles.add_emitter`, `particles.set_emitter`, `particles.remove_emitter`, `particles.kill_emitter_particles`, `particles.clear`, texture helpers, and count queries), and also exposes `particles.emit_burst({...})` for one-shot effects. Burst options use the normal emitter vocabulary (`position`, `velocity`, `color_start`, `color_end`, `count`, `size_min`, `size_max`, `life_min`, `life_max`, `spawn_radius`, `noise_strength`, `drag`, `texture_index`, `orientation`) plus optional `preset` and `cleanup_delay`; the built-in hit presets are `hero_take`, `hero_give`, `enemy_take`, and `enemy_give`. The helper marks burst emitters with a negative spawn rate, and the particle compute shader fires those particles once using the emitter token in `position.w`. `ParticleManager::Update()` only physically removes expired transients from the emitter-list tail, so cleanup never shifts the particle offsets of any later persistent or transient emitter. Expired transients stranded behind active emitters remain allocated until the tail clears or until the next same-count `emit_burst` reuses their slot with a fresh one-shot token; mismatched-count bursts append instead of shifting downstream particle ranges. Old particles age out on the GPU and the token check prevents accidental re-firing. The particle GPU buffer is retained and grows geometrically in 1,024-particle-aligned steps instead of reallocating at every small high-water mark or shrinking during burst cleanup; growth swaps to the larger buffer immediately and retires the old in-flight buffer through the owning frame's deletion queue instead of draining the whole GPU queue. Per-frame emitter buffers start with room for 128 emitters and double when exceeded, preventing three swapchain-local allocations whenever combat adds one emitter. The emitter metadata uploaded to the GPU is copied into those per-swapchain-frame buffers before the compute/draw passes bind descriptors, so heavy Lua emitter churn cannot rewrite or destroy a buffer still referenced by an in-flight command list. Particle-range clears requested by Lua/update are queued and flushed through the particle compute command buffer for the same reason.

Native bursts (script ABI 24, verified 2026-10-01): `phasma::ParticleBurst` keeps its v10 prefix and appends `size` and `stretch`; with `BurstStretch` (bit 13) set, `CppScript.cpp` reads the tail only when the module is ABI 24+ and `size` covers it, and rejects a non-finite or negative stretch. `ParticleManager::MakeBurstEmitter` stores the stretch (seconds of velocity) in `emitter.velocity.w`; `Particle/ParticleVS.hlsl` draws such particles camera-plane, elongated along their velocity to speed x stretch, clamped between one and eight diameters (`2*size..16*size`, `size` being the radius), and keeps them camera-facing when the velocity points into the camera. Soft particles: `ParticlePass.cpp` binds the scene `depthStencil` SRV in set 1 and `Particle/ParticlePS.hlsl` discards fragments behind the depth and fades alpha over the last 10 cm in front of a surface (perspective and orthographic). DX12 pitfall seen that day: the backend maps `[[vk::binding(N,S)]]` to `register(tN, spaceS)` with BindPoint == binding (`Dx12ShaderImpl.cpp`), so a texture bound after `Texture2D textures[16]` at binding 0 must use binding 16 or higher; `sceneDepth` at binding 2 failed DXIL compilation and stopped the DX12 player at start-up (open at the time of writing). The ATH project keeps byte-identical copies under `Assets/Shaders/Particle/`.
